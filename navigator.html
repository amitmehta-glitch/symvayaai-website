What the 2026 research says, and what it leaves out.

Eighteen studies on Indian enterprise AI came out in the past year. They report “adoption” anywhere from 21% to nearly 100%, because each defines it differently. Most are vendor-sponsored, ask only technology leaders, and lean towards large enterprises and GCCs.

What CXOs still lack is profit-and-loss grade evidence of value; the views of the CFO, CHRO, CMO and COO alongside the CIO; the real running cost of AI agents; and data on the mid-market, public sector undertakings and family-owned groups. That is the gap Navigator’s research is built to close.

The India AI Readiness Benchmark

An annual study of how ready Indian organisations are to take AI from pilot to measurable value. Three to five leaders from each organisation answer 33 short questions, in about 15 minutes each. Their answers are combined into one view per organisation.

  • Your readiness report. Journey readiness from 0 to 100, where your AI work stalls, and a Now, Next, Later roadmap.
  • A leadership alignment readout. Where your leaders score the same capability two or more levels apart, and where nobody knows.
  • Your position against peers. Where you stand against organisations in your industry group and size band, stage by stage.
  • The first look. Each finding at least a week before public release, and an invitation to a closed-door roundtable.

Answers are confidential. No figure is shown for a group of fewer than 5 organisations or published for fewer than 10, and there is no vendor pitch in the assessment or the report.
